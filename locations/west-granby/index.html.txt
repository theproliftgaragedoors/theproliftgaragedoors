  <h2 id="services">Garage door services we provide in West Granby</h2>
  <h3>Garage door spring replacement</h3>
  <p>Springs counterbalance the weight of the door, which can be well over 100 pounds and far more for a solid wood carriage door. A torsion spring is rated for a limited number of open-and-close cycles. When one breaks you will often hear a loud bang, and the door then feels extremely heavy or will not lift. We replace springs in matched pairs where appropriate, match them to the door's real weight and height, and test the balance afterward. Never attempt this yourself; the stored energy is dangerous.</p>
  <h3>Cable repair and roller replacement</h3>
  <p>Cables carry the load when the door moves. Frayed, rusty or snapped cables can leave one side of the door hanging crooked, and worn rollers cause grinding and shaking. We replace cables in pairs and upgrade worn rollers to quieter, longer-lasting options when it makes sense.</p>
  <h3>Off-track and bent-track repair</h3>
  <p>A door that has jumped its track is unsafe to operate, and forcing it can bend panels and track sections beyond repair. We secure the door, straighten or replace damaged track, realign the system and run full cycles before we leave.</p>
  <h3>Garage door opener repair and installation</h3>
  <p>If your opener hums but does not move the door, clicks, stalls, reverses or ignores the remote, we check power, logic board, travel limits, safety sensors, drive system and mounting hardware. When replacement is the better value, we install chain, belt and direct-drive models with Wi-Fi control, battery backup and quiet operation, and program remotes and keypads on the spot.</p>
  <h3>New garage door installation</h3>
  <p>Tired of dents, rust or a door that never seals? We measure, supply and install new doors in steel, insulated steel, wood and carriage-house styles, along with new tracks, springs, weatherstripping and openers where needed.</p>

  <h2 id="signs">Signs your West Granby garage door needs repair</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Loud bang, then the door will not lift:</strong> usually a broken torsion spring.</li>
    <li><strong>Door sits crooked or one side hangs lower:</strong> a snapped or slipped cable, or a bent track.</li>
    <li><strong>Grinding, squealing or shaking:</strong> worn rollers, dry hinges or loose hardware.</li>

    <li><strong>Remote only works up close:</strong> a weak battery, failing receiver or interference.</li>

  <h2 id="local-issues">Local garage door problems in West Granby</h2>
  <h3>Heavy snow, ice storms and hard freezes</h3>
  <p>West Granby sits on higher, open ground in the hills of northern Hartford County, so winter can bring heavy wet snow, drifting wind, ice and long stretches below freezing. Cold stiffens grease and rubber seals, contracts metal and loads springs and openers. A bottom seal can freeze to the concrete overnight, and an opener may strain against it until a gear or limit setting gives way. If your door sticks on a frosty morning, do not keep pressing the button; clear snow and ice from the base, inspect the seal, then call us.</p>
  <h3>Storm outages on rural power lines</h3>
  <p>Tree-lined country roads and overhead lines make outages after wind and ice storms a real possibility. Every opener has a red emergency release cord for manual operation. If the door is stuck or the cord does not work, call us rather than forcing it. A battery-backup opener is a worthwhile upgrade where the garage is the main entrance.</p>
  <h3>Farm, barn and horse-property garages</h3>
  <p>Many West Granby properties have detached barns, equipment sheds and oversized openings. These often use heavy wood doors, wide or tall openings and limited headroom. We measure carefully and select springs and hardware to suit the actual opening rather than forcing a standard kit. Dust, hay chaff and moisture also shorten the life of openers and sensors in working buildings.</p>
  <h3>Long driveways and weak remote signal</h3>
  <p>Long driveways, stone walls and hilly terrain can weaken remote and Wi-Fi signals. If a smart opener drops offline or a remote works only when you are close, we can test the receiver, relocate an antenna, replace batteries or recommend a model with better range.</p>
  <h3>Wooded lots, leaves and wildlife</h3>
  <p>Leaves, pine needles and debris collect in tracks and at sensor lenses, and rodents sometimes chew wiring or nest near openers in unheated garages. If your sensors blink or your opener behaves erratically, schedule an inspection before a minor wiring issue becomes a failed logic board.</p>
  <h3>Road salt and winter debris</h3>
  <p>Salt and sand tracked into the garage accelerate corrosion on the bottom bracket, bottom panel and weather seal. Rinse the bottom of the door and the floor edge periodically in winter.</p>

  <h2 id="howto">How to check and care for your garage door (step by step)</h2>
  <h3>How to test your garage door balance</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Close the door fully, then pull the red emergency release cord to disconnect the opener.</li>
    <li>Lift the door by hand to about halfway open.</li>
    <li>Let go. A balanced door stays put. If it drops or shoots up, the springs are worn or out of adjustment.</li>
    <li>Reconnect the opener only after the door is closed, and call a technician for any imbalance. Do not touch the springs or cables.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to test the safety reversal system</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Place a roll of paper towels or a 2x4 flat on the floor in the path of the door.</li>
    <li>Press the button to close the door.</li>
    <li>The door should reverse as soon as it touches the object. If it does not, stop using the opener and call us.</li>
    <li>Wave a broom handle through the sensor beam while the door closes; it should reverse immediately.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to lubricate your garage door</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Wipe dirt from hinges, springs and roller stems.</li>
    <li>Apply a garage-door-specific lubricant to hinges, roller bearings and the spring coil.</li>
    <li>Do not grease the tracks; clean them with a dry cloth instead.</li>
    <li>Cycle the door a few times and wipe off any excess.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to open your door manually during a power outage</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Make sure the door is fully closed if possible and no one is under it.</li>
    <li>Pull the red cord down and back to release the trolley.</li>
    <li>Lift the door carefully by the handle. If it feels very heavy, the spring may be broken: stop and call a professional.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to winterize your garage door before the first freeze</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Clean the tracks and bottom edge of the door, then check the bottom seal for cracks or gaps.</li>
    <li>Lubricate hinges, roller bearings and the spring coil.</li>
    <li>Run the balance and safety-reversal tests above.</li>
    <li>Replace remote and keypad batteries, and consider battery backup if outages are common on your road.</li>
    <li>Book a professional tune-up if the door is over ten years old or you notice noise, shaking or slow travel.</li>
  </ol>
