  <h3>Garage door spring replacement</h3>
  <p>Springs counterbalance the weight of the door, which can be well over 100 pounds, and far more for a solid wood or oversized door. When a spring breaks you will often hear a loud bang from the garage, and the door then feels extremely heavy or will not lift at all. We replace springs in matched pairs where appropriate, match the spring to the door's actual weight and height, and test the balance afterward. Never attempt this yourself; the stored energy is dangerous.</p>

  <h2 id="howto">How to check and care for your garage door (step by step)</h2>
  <h3>How to test your garage door balance</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Close the door fully, then pull the red emergency release cord to disconnect the opener.</li>
    <li>Lift the door by hand to about halfway open.</li>
    <li>Let go. A balanced door stays put. If it drops or shoots up, the springs are worn or out of adjustment.</li>
    <li>Reconnect the opener only after the door is closed, and call a technician for any imbalance. Do not touch the springs or cables.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to test the safety reversal system</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Place a roll of paper towels or a 2x4 flat on the floor in the path of the door.</li>
    <li>Press the button to close the door.</li>
    <li>The door should reverse as soon as it touches the object. If it does not, stop using the opener and call us.</li>
    <li>Also wave a broom handle through the sensor beam while the door closes; it should reverse immediately.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to lubricate your garage door</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Wipe dirt, dust and chaff from hinges, springs and roller stems.</li>
    <li>Apply a garage-door-specific lubricant (silicone or lithium-based) to hinges, roller bearings and the spring coil.</li>
    <li>Do not grease the tracks themselves; clean them with a dry cloth instead.</li>
    <li>Open and close the door a few times and wipe off any excess.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to open your door manually during a power outage</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Make sure the door is fully closed if possible and no one is under it.</li>
    <li>Pull the red cord down and back to release the trolley.</li>
    <li>Lift the door carefully by the handle. If it feels very heavy, the spring may be broken: stop and call a professional.</li>
  </ol>
  <h3>How to winterize your garage door before the first freeze</h3>
  <ol>
    <li>Clean the tracks and the bottom edge of the door, then check the bottom seal for cracks or gaps.</li>
    <li>Lubricate hinges, roller bearings and the spring coil with a garage-door-specific product.</li>
    <li>Run the balance and safety-reversal tests described above.</li>
    <li>Replace remote and keypad batteries, and consider a battery-backup opener if outages are common on your road.</li>
    <li>Book a professional tune-up if the door is more than ten years old or you notice noise, shaking or slow travel.</li>
