Choosing the right door for a Connecticut home

Material matters more in Connecticut than in milder climates. Insulated steel is the workhorse — durable, low-maintenance, and available in wood-look finishes that suit period homes from Greenwich to Litchfield. Composite and faux-wood overlays give the carriage-house look without the refinishing that real wood demands. Solid wood is beautiful on historic homes but needs regular maintenance. Aluminum and glass suit contemporary homes but insulate poorly unless thermally broken. We'll talk through how you use the garage — daily entry, workshop, storage, living space above — because that decides how much insulation and durability actually matter for you.

What a proper installation includes

A garage door installation is not just hanging panels. The tracks must be level and plumb, the springs matched to the door's exact weight, the cables wound evenly, and the opener's travel and force settings calibrated to the new door. Shortcuts show up quickly: a door that binds, an opener that strains, seals that don't meet the floor. Our technicians install the complete system — door, tracks, springs, hardware, weather seals — and test the balance and safety reversal before calling the job done. We take the old door away with us, and we leave the garage clean.

How long installation takes

A standard single-door installation usually takes a full day, including removing the old door. Double doors and doors with custom features take longer. We'll give you a clear schedule when we measure, and we'll confirm the timing when you book. Installation is available Monday through Saturday across Connecticut, and we can often coordinate with your builder or contractor on new construction.
