Signs You Need It
Signs You Need
Garage Door Balancing

Catching these early usually means a smaller repair instead of an emergency call. If any of these sound familiar, call us — we'll tell you honestly whether it needs a visit.

Door won't stay halfway

Disconnect the opener and lift the door halfway — a balanced door stays put. If it drifts down or flies up, the springs need adjusting.

Door heavy to lift by hand

A healthy door lifts easily with one hand. If it feels heavy, the springs aren't carrying their share of the weight.

Opener straining or overheating

An unbalanced door forces the motor to lift weight the springs should carry — the most common cause of premature opener failure.

Door slams or drifts

A door that slams shut at the bottom or drifts open has springs set too loose or too tight for its weight.

Uneven movement

One side rising faster than the other usually means uneven spring tension or a cable problem on one side.

Opener reversing for no reason

An unbalanced door can trip the opener's force limits, making it reverse as if it hit an obstruction.

What a balanced door feels like

Try this: disconnect the opener with the red release cord, then lift the door by hand. A balanced door rises smoothly with one hand, stays wherever you stop it — a quarter open, halfway, three-quarters — and lowers gently without slamming. That's the springs doing their job: counterbalancing the door's full weight so the opener only has to guide it. When the balance drifts, the door gets heavy, the opener strains, and everything wears faster — springs, cables, rollers, and the opener itself. Balance is the single most important thing about how a garage door feels and how long its parts last.

Why doors go out of balance

Springs lose tension gradually as they age, and they don't always lose it evenly — one spring in a pair can weaken faster than the other, leaving the door lifting unevenly. Cables stretch slightly over time. A new panel, new insulation, or a layer of paint can change the door's weight enough to shift the balance. And after any spring or cable work, the balance must be checked and set — it's part of the job, not an optional extra. We check the balance on every visit, because a door that's even slightly out of balance is wearing out its opener.

Why balance matters for safety

An unbalanced door is a safety issue as well as a wear issue. A door that slams shut can injure someone underneath it. A door that won't stay open can drop while someone is working under it. And an opener straining against an unbalanced door can burn out its motor or strip its gears without warning, leaving the door stuck. If your door feels heavy, drifts, or slams, don't wait — balancing is a quick job, and it protects everything else in the system.
