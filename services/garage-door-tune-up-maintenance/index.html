Signs You Need It
Signs You Need
Tune-Up & Maintenance

Catching these early usually means a smaller repair instead of an emergency call. If any of these sound familiar, call us — we'll tell you honestly whether it needs a visit.

Door getting noisy

Grinding, squeaking, or rattling that wasn't there last year usually means dry hardware or worn rollers — both easy fixes when caught early.

Slower than it used to be

A door that takes longer to open or close is often fighting dry tracks, worn rollers, or springs losing tension.

Small issues adding up

A squeak here, a slight rattle there — small problems compound, and a tune-up addresses them all in one visit.

Door hasn't been serviced in years

If the door has never had a professional inspection, there will be things to find. Most are quick fixes.

Seasonal check before winter

Cold weather thickens lubrication and stresses springs. A tune-up before winter prevents the cold-morning breakdown.

Buying or selling a home

A tune-up with a full inspection gives buyers confidence and sellers one less thing to worry about.

What a tune-up actually covers

A ProLift tune-up is a full inspection of the door system, not a squirt of lubricant and a handshake. We check the springs for fatigue and correct tension, the cables for fraying and rust, the rollers for wear, the hinges for cracks, the tracks for alignment, the drums and bearings for wear, and every bolt and bracket for tightness. Then we lubricate the moving parts, test the door's balance, check the opener's travel and force settings, and test the safety sensors and auto-reverse. You get a plain report of what we found — what's fine, what needs attention now, and what to watch.

Why annual maintenance matters

A garage door is the largest moving system in most homes, and it cycles hundreds of times a year. Springs fatigue, cables fray, rollers wear, and hardware works loose — all gradually, all silently, until something fails. Most of the emergency calls we get are for problems a tune-up would have caught months earlier: a spring about to snap, a cable about to fray through, a roller about to seize. Annual maintenance doesn't just prevent breakdowns — it extends the life of every part in the system, including the opener.

The best time for a tune-up

Autumn is ideal: lubrication is fresh before the cold, springs are checked before the temperature drops, and weather seals are inspected before winter drafts arrive. But any time is better than never — if your door hasn't been serviced in two years or more, it's overdue. A tune-up takes about an hour, and it's available Monday through Saturday across Connecticut.
