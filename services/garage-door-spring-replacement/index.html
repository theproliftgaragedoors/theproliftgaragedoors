Signs You Need It
Signs You Need
Spring Replacement

Catching these early usually means a smaller repair instead of an emergency call. If any of these sound familiar, call us — we'll tell you honestly whether it needs a visit.

A loud bang from the garage

A torsion spring snapping sounds like a gunshot. If you heard it and the door won't open, the spring is almost certainly the cause.

Door won't open, or only opens a few inches

The opener can't lift a door with no spring assistance. It will strain, stop, and reverse — or burn out trying.

Door feels very heavy by hand

A healthy door lifts easily with one hand. If it suddenly feels like it weighs a hundred pounds, a spring has failed.

A visible gap in the spring

Look above the door: a torsion spring with a gap of a few inches in the coil has snapped. Extension springs show a stretched or broken coil along the track.

Cables hanging loose or slack

When a spring breaks, the cables lose tension and can slip off the drums — a second problem that needs attention at the same time.

Door opens crooked or unevenly

One side lifting before the other usually means one of a pair of springs has failed while the other still pulls.

Why springs fail — and why they matter so much

A garage door spring does nearly all the lifting. The opener only guides the door; the springs counterbalance its full weight, which can be 150 pounds or more. Every open-and-close cycle wears the spring a little, and after roughly 10,000 cycles — about seven to ten years of normal use — the steel fatigues and snaps. Cold Connecticut winters make it worse: metal contracts, lubrication thickens, and a spring that was already near the end of its life often lets go on the coldest morning of the year. When it breaks, the door becomes dead weight, and the opener is neither designed nor safe to lift it alone.

Torsion springs vs. extension springs

Most modern doors use torsion springs mounted on a shaft above the door; older doors often use extension springs that stretch along the horizontal tracks. The two types fail differently and are replaced differently. Torsion springs are wound to a precise tension matched to the door's weight — too little tension and the door is heavy, too much and it flies up and strains the opener. Extension springs need safety cables running through them, so that if one snaps it can't fly across the garage. Our technicians carry springs for standard residential doors on the truck and can source heavy-duty or high-cycle springs for larger or commercial doors.

Never a DIY job

A wound torsion spring stores enough energy to cause serious injury — this is the repair emergency rooms see most often from garage doors. Winding bars, the correct spring for the door's weight, and knowing how many turns to apply are not things to learn from a video. If your spring has broken, don't try to force the door open, don't keep running the opener, and don't try to unwind or remove the spring yourself. Call us, keep people and vehicles clear of the door, and we'll have it working again the same day in most cases.
